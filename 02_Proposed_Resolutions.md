# 2026 SBC Proposed Resolutions

**Prepared for the 2026 Southern Baptist Convention Annual Meeting**
**Orlando, Florida | June 9-10, 2026**

## Resolution 1: On the 250th Anniversary of the United States and the Baptist Contribution to Religious Liberty

### Whereas Clauses

1. The United States was founded on July 4, 1776, with the Declaration of Independence recognizing that all people are created equal and endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable rights, establishing a nation committed to ordered liberty and the rule of law.
2. The American founders understood these rights are given by God and secured by government and comprehended the importance of Christianity to the framing and flourishing of the new republic (Romans 13:1-7).
3. The freedoms enshrined in the US Constitution and Bill of Rights -- particularly freedom of religion and freedom of speech -- have enabled free and open proclamation of the gospel at home and abroad.
4. Baptist leaders such as Isaac Backus, John Leland, and others played a vital role in advocating for religious liberty, contributing to the development and adoption of the First Amendment, which guarantees the free exercise of religion and prohibits establishment of a state church.
5. Baptists have historically affirmed that religious liberty is not merely a political principle but a biblical conviction rooted in the teaching that every individual is accountable to God and must be free to respond to Him without coercion from the state (Matthew 22:21; Acts 4:19-20, 17:26-27; Romans 14:12).
6. 2026 marks the semiquincentennial (250th anniversary) of the founding of the United States.
7. We recognize the many challenges and shortcomings in our national history, including sins such as slavery, racism, abortion, injustice, and sexual immorality, and affirm the continuing need for national repentance, reconciliation, and revival through the gospel.
8. Despite its failures, the United States increasingly realized the promise of its founding by ending slavery and segregation at home and defending freedom from 20th-century totalitarian threats of fascism and communism abroad.
9. Scripture exhorts believers to honor governing authorities, to seek the welfare of the cities and nations in which they dwell, and to pray for kings and all in authority (Jeremiah 29:7; Romans 13:1; 1 Timothy 2:1-2).
10. The semiquincentennial provides an opportunity for Southern Baptists to express gratitude to God for His blessings, to renew commitment to faithfully proclaim the gospel, and to stand for truth, righteousness, and justice in the public square.

### Resolved

1. Give thanks to Almighty God for his providential hand in the founding and sustaining of the United States over the past 250 years.
2. Urge Southern Baptists and all faithful Christians to pray for spiritual revival across the nation.
3. Honor the legacy of Baptist forebears who courageously contended for religious liberty and helped secure the freedoms enshrined in the First Amendment.
4. Reaffirm our historic Baptist commitment to religious liberty for all people, recognizing it as a God-given right grounded in the dignity of every human being made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27-28).
5. Call upon Southern Baptists to steward these freedoms by proclaiming the gospel, living as faithful citizens of both the kingdom of God and the United States, and engaging the public square with courage and civility.
6. Call upon Southern Baptists to pursue national renewal through biblically informed civic engagement, including advocating for just laws rooted in God's natural law and consistent with Scripture, and electing public officials who will do the same.
7. Urge our churches to pray for national, state, and local leaders, that we may live peaceful and godly lives (1 Timothy 2:2).
8. Commit ourselves anew to the Great Commission, recognizing that the ultimate hope for America and all nations is in the redeeming work of Jesus Christ, whose kingdom will never end.

## Resolution 3: On the Church's Opportunity for Evangelism, Discipleship, and Care for Persons with Disabilities and Their Families

### Whereas Clauses

1. Scripture clearly teaches and The Baptist Faith and Message 2000 affirms that every human being is made in the image of God and therefore possesses inherent dignity and worth, undiminished by physical, intellectual, or developmental differences (Genesis 1:26-27; Psalm 139:13-16; James 3:9).
2. God the Father desires that all would come to a saving relationship with Jesus Christ, and Jesus commands us to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19-20).
3. Families of children, youth, and adults with physical, developmental, and intellectual disabilities often face persistent challenges, including social isolation and the physical exhaustion of caregiving.
4. Research suggests that families of those with disabilities are significantly less likely to attend church regularly due to a lack of accessibility, programming, or sense of belonging.
5. Jesus calls us to welcome, love, protect, honor, and serve those devalued and pushed to the margins of society (Leviticus 19:14; Luke 14:12-14; Romans 14:13; 1 Corinthians 12:22-26).
6. Every Southern Baptist church has an opportunity to extend love, hospitality, and pastoral care to those inside the church and gospel witness to those outside the church by providing or expanding ministry to those with disabilities and their families.
7. The Southern Baptist Convention Executive Committee's Disability Ministry Task Force, reporting at the 2026 Annual Meeting, calls each church and SBC entity to the same.

### Resolved

1. Call upon churches to welcome joyfully every person regardless of physical, developmental, or intellectual capacity.
2. Call upon Southern Baptists to model speech that is compassionate, truthful, and honoring to fellow image bearers.
3. Urge Southern Baptist churches of every size and in every community to develop or expand plans for inclusion of those with disabilities so that no family is forced to choose between the particular needs of a family member and the fellowship of the church.
4. Encourage churches to evaluate their facilities to identify barriers to access for those with physical limitations and to act with hospitality to reduce or eliminate those barriers to the greatest degree practical (Mark 2:1-12).
5. Exhort our seminaries, entities, state conventions, and associations to highlight the need for churches and their leaders to have training, skills, and resources needed to care compassionately for families navigating the complexities of special needs.
6. Affirm that by providing excellent care for those with disabilities and their families, our churches will be a faithful witness of Jesus Christ to a watching world.

## Resolution 4: On Assisted Suicide and the Sanctity of Life

### Whereas Clauses

1. The Bible teaches that God, the Author of life, created all human beings in his image and declares human life to be sacred and worthy of protection from conception until natural death (Genesis 1:26-27; Exodus 20:13; Job 14:5; Psalm 139:13,16; Jeremiah 1:5; Acts 17:28; James 3:9).
2. This Convention has previously warned against the emergence of a "culture of death," and recent legal and cultural developments demonstrate that this concern remains urgently relevant ("Resolution on Assisted Suicide," 1996).
3. The Southern Baptist Convention has consistently affirmed in resolutions adopted in 1992, 1996, and 2001 the sanctity of human life and has opposed euthanasia and assisted suicide.
4. In the years since those resolutions, the practice of assisted suicide has expanded significantly and is now legal in at least thirteen states and the District of Columbia, with most of these laws enacted in the past decade.
5. Since the last annual meeting, additional states, including some of the nation's largest, have enacted or announced plans to enact such laws.
6. Advocates of these laws often appeal to concepts such as autonomy, compassion, dignity, and the alleviation of suffering, reframing assisted suicide as a form of medical care.
7. Experience in other countries demonstrates that such practices do not remain limited but expand over time, as seen in Canada and European nations such as the Netherlands.
8. These developments confirm that so-called safeguards are often temporary, as worldviews that reject a biblical view of personhood ultimately erode meaningful limits on the taking of human life.
9. Scripture recognizes both the evil of personal suffering and the good of God's redemptive purposes in it (Romans 5:3-4; 2 Corinthians 1:9, 12:7-10; James 1:2-4).
10. The intentional ending of innocent human life is contrary to biblical teaching and undermines the dignity and worth of every human being.

### Resolved

1. Reaffirm our unwavering commitment to the sanctity of human life from conception until natural death.
2. Reaffirm continued opposition to euthanasia and assisted suicide in all its forms, including practices now described as "medical aid in dying" and "death with dignity."
3. Reject the rebranding of assisted suicide through euphemistic language that obscures the moral reality of intentionally ending human life.
4. Warn that the legalization of assisted suicide leads to the expansion of such practices, placing the elderly, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable populations at increased risk.
5. Call upon federal, state, and local governments to uphold laws that protect human life and to resist efforts to legalize or expand assisted suicide.
6. Urge medical professionals, healthcare institutions, and policymakers to prioritize ethical care that affirms the dignity of every patient, including effective pain management, hospice care, and palliative support.
7. Call upon Southern Baptist pastors, churches, and families to provide compassionate, faithful presence to those who are suffering physically and mentally (Romans 12:15-16; Galatians 6:2; Philippians 2:4).
8. Commit to bearing clear and faithful witness in the public square to a biblical ethic of life (2 Corinthians 4:6; 1 Peter 3:15).

## Resolution 5: On Political Violence and Speech

### Whereas Clauses

1. Scripture teaches that every human being is made in the image of God and therefore possesses inherent dignity and worth, and that the taking of innocent life is an offense against the Creator (Genesis 1:26-27, 9:6; Exodus 20:13; James 3:9).
2. Our Lord Jesus Christ commanded his followers to love their neighbors, to love even their enemies, to pray for those who persecute them, to pursue peace with all people, and to overcome evil with good (Matthew 5:9,38-48, 22:39; Luke 6:27-36; Romans 12:14-21).
3. The Baptist Faith and Message 2000 states that Christians should "contend for the sanctity of all human life," that "it is the duty of Christians to seek peace with all men on principles of righteousness," that "the true remedy for the war spirit is the gospel of our Lord," and that Christians should "pray for the reign of the Prince of Peace" (Articles XV, XVI).
4. The use or threat of violence for political ends -- including assassinations and attempted assassinations, harassment and murder in houses of worship, vandalism of crisis pregnancy centers, and public mayhem -- assaults the sanctity of human life, the rule of law, ordered liberty, and freedom of conscience.
5. Dehumanizing rhetoric, reckless accusations, slander, and the normalization of hatred and contempt can harden consciences, inflame passions, and make violence more palatable in public life (Proverbs 10:18, 12:18, 16:28; Ephesians 4:29-32; James 3:5-10).
6. Scripture calls Christians to speak the truth in love, to be quick to hear and slow to anger, to serve as ministers of reconciliation, and to display the fruit of the Spirit even amid disagreement (2 Corinthians 5:18-20; Galatians 5:22-23; Ephesians 4:3,15; James 1:19-20).
7. Polarization becomes toxic when spirited disagreements shift from biblical convictions to spurious judgments about the character, motives, or humanity of opponents, fostering suspicion, contempt, fear, and hostility.
8. The church's weapons are not carnal but spiritual, and The Baptist Faith and Message 2000 affirms that Christians are to advance the gospel through faithful proclamation, principles of righteousness, truth, and brotherly love rather than through coercion or violence (Article XV; 2 Corinthians 10:3-5).
9. Faithful Christian citizenship includes engagement in public life through voting, advocacy, peaceful assembly, lawful protest, and other upright means, while refusing political idolatry (Jeremiah 29:7; Matthew 22:21; Romans 13:1-7; 1 Timothy 2:1-4).

### Resolved

1. Unequivocally condemn political violence in all its forms -- including assassinations, terrorism, rioting, mob intimidation, vandalism, threats, and coercion -- as morally evil, contrary to the sanctity of human life, and incompatible with the way of Christ, regardless of ideology, identity, party, or cause.
2. Reject any claim that righteous ends justify unrighteous means, affirming that the cause of truth is never served by lies, justice is never served by lawlessness, and life is never served by murder.
3. Reject hatred, malice, slander, dehumanization, intimidation, reckless speech, and contemptible conduct as inconsistent with Christian discipleship, while also rejecting a false peace that refuses necessary truth.
4. Call Southern Baptists to examine our own speech, online conduct, and public witness, refusing to treat image bearers as enemies to be destroyed rather than neighbors to be loved.
5. Urge pastors, churches, parents, and ministry leaders to disciple believers toward faithful Christian citizenship marked by truthfulness, moral clarity, courage, humility, love of neighbor, patience, prayer, and self-control.
6. Call on elected officials, candidates, political parties, media figures, and all public leaders to condemn political violence consistently and protect the lives and safety of citizens, public servants, and houses of worship.
7. Affirm the important role of law enforcement to maintain civil order as servants of God who punish evil and protect the innocent (Romans 13:4).
8. Commit to pray diligently for governing authorities at every level, including those with whom we disagree (1 Timothy 2:1-2).
9. Reaffirm that the church's mission is advanced not by force but by the proclamation of the gospel, the ministry of the Word, prayer, and the visible witness of regenerate believers.
10. Commit to live as ambassadors for Christ and peacemakers in a divided world, proclaiming the gospel of reconciliation as the only true hope for individuals, churches, and nations until Christ returns.

## Resolution 6: On Antisemitism

### Whereas Clauses

1. Since the Hamas terrorist attacks of October 7, 2023, antisemitism has surged across the United States and the world, manifesting in physical violence, harassment, university campus glorification of terrorism, and broad aggression directed at Jewish people.
2. This rising hatred includes conspiracy theories perpetuated online and through social media, falsely accusing the Jewish people of controlling media, finance, politics, weather, and culture -- modern echoes of historic libels that have incited persecution for centuries.
3. Such conspiracy theories and condemnations of the Jewish people are contrary to the truth and the clear teaching of Scripture regarding human dignity.
4. Southern Baptists have historically expressed profound gratitude for the Jewish people and have consistently condemned antisemitism as unchristian and contrary to the gospel.
5. God chose Abraham and his descendants for his redemptive purpose; to them he gave the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the law, the worship, and the promises, all fulfilled in the person and work of the Lord Jesus Christ (Genesis 12, 15, 17; Luke 24; Romans 9:4-5).
6. God maintains his purpose for and promise to the Jewish people as revealed in Scripture; salvation is "from the Jews" (John 4:22); the gospel is "the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek" (Romans 1:16-17).

### Resolved

1. Unequivocally condemn this new surge of antisemitism in all its forms -- including violence, cultural hatred, and conspiracy theories of Jewish controlled cabals -- as sinful, unchristian, and an assault on both biblical truth and basic human dignity.
2. Stand unashamedly with the Jewish people against all such hatred, including all acts of terror against them worldwide.
3. Reaffirm our love for the Jewish people as those "loved because of the patriarchs" (Romans 11:28), and our commitment to proclaim the gospel of Jesus Christ to them with urgency, respect, and boldness.
4. Call upon Southern Baptists to combat antisemitism in our churches and communities through biblical teaching, personal friendship with Jewish neighbors, courageous public witness, and prayer for the peace of Jerusalem and the salvation of Israel (Psalm 122:6).
5. Affirm God's love and offer of salvation through Jesus Christ to all people without distinction, while rejecting every form of ethnic or religious hatred and upholding religious liberty for all, and praying for the day when "all Israel will be saved" (Romans 11:26).

## Resolution 7: On Immigration, Human Dignity, and the Rule of Law

### Whereas Clauses

1. God created every human being in his image and calls his people to show justice, mercy, hospitality, and love toward the vulnerable and the stranger (Genesis 1:26-27; Deuteronomy 10:18-19; Leviticus 19:34; Matthew 25:35; Ephesians 2:14-19).
2. For more than eighty years the Southern Baptist Convention has addressed refugees, displaced persons, immigrants, and those fleeing war and persecution by affirming both compassionate ministry and the responsibility of governments to maintain lawful order, secure borders, and implement humane immigration systems (Isaiah 1:17; Micah 6:8; Romans 13:1-7).
3. Southern Baptists reject racism, nativism, ethnic supremacy, and dehumanizing rhetoric, affirming that Scripture alone defines our identity, worth, and unity in Christ (Galatians 3:28; Colossians 4:6; James 2:1-4).
4. Large-scale and often disorderly migration has strained communities, harmed migrants and American citizens, and diminished confidence in the rule of law, underscoring the need for immigration systems marked by order, clarity, due process, timely adjudication, and humane enforcement.
5. While immigration through means that violate American law has been and continues to be a problem, many immigrants and refugees have carefully followed proper procedures for entering the nation lawfully.
6. Southern Baptist churches include men and women of numerous ethnicities and are positioned to bear faithful gospel witness through ministries of hospitality, discipleship, practical service, and Christ-centered community.
7. Leaders representing diverse Southern Baptist ethnic fellowships have recently affirmed the importance of compassionate and lawful immigration policies, dignifying language, religious liberty, and gospel-centered ministry to immigrant and refugee communities.

### Resolved

1. Reaffirm our longstanding conviction that every human being possesses God-given worth and dignity as an image-bearer of God (Genesis 1:26-27).
2. Affirm that the church is called to faithful gospel proclamation, love of neighbor, hospitality to the stranger, and making disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:18-20; Luke 10:25-37).
3. Reaffirm the call to pray for governing authorities as they carry out the responsibility to secure borders, enforce immigration laws, maintain an orderly immigration system, and pursue clear, enforceable, and humane policies that uphold both justice and mercy (Micah 6:8; Romans 13:1-7; 1 Timothy 2:1-2).
4. Reaffirm support for lawful immigration enforcement carried out justly, humanely, and according to due process, including the removal of those whom the government duly prioritizes for deportation.
5. Reject amnesty, understood as forgiveness of legal violations without accountability.
6. Reject nativism, racial or ethnic hostility, ethno-nationalism, discrimination, and all ideologies or rhetoric that deny the equal worth and dignity of any people group regardless of immigration status.
7. Exhort the government to take particular care of children and families, insisting upon respect for the God-given worth and dignity of every person and the religious liberty of churches to minister freely.
8. Encourage Southern Baptist churches to strengthen faithful gospel ministry among immigrants and refugees.

## Resolution 8: On Finishing Well in Life and Ministry

### Whereas Clauses

1. The grace of God has appeared, both "bringing salvation for all people" and "instructing us to deny godlessness and worldly lusts and to live in a sensible, righteous, and godly way in the present age" (Titus 2:11-12).
2. Those called to pastoral ministry are called to a noble task, requiring them to live the Christian life above reproach, characterized by holiness, self-control, and integrity, and to guard their lives and their teaching carefully (1 Timothy 3:1-7, 4:16; Titus 1:5-9).
3. The apostle Paul testifies near the end of his life, "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" (2 Timothy 4:7), setting before all believers and especially ministers of the gospel a vision of faithful endurance to the end.
4. The New Testament regularly instructs the people of God to imitate the faith of those leaders who exemplify lives transformed by the gospel (1 Corinthians 4:16, 11:1; Philippians 3:17; 1 Thessalonians 1:6; 2 Thessalonians 3:7-9; Hebrews 13:7).
5. Hebrews 11 provides testimony of a cloud of witnesses -- men and women who endured to the end by faith, believing that God's promises are better than the fleeting pleasures of sin.
6. The Christian life is likened to a race in which Christians must lay aside every weight and sin and run with endurance the race that is set before them, looking to Jesus, the author and perfecter of their faith (1 Corinthians 9:27; Hebrews 12:1-2).
7. In our day what often gets most public attention is pastors and ministry leaders who have fallen into sin and moral failure, who have hindered churches with scandal, or who have been divisive and unwise in public discourse.
8. Contrary to these negative examples, countless positive examples of pastors and ministry leaders across the Southern Baptist Convention have served for decades with integrity and steadfastness.

### Resolved

1. Express heartfelt gratitude to the Lord for pastors and ministry leaders who, by his grace, labored faithfully over many years and finished well, keeping the faith and maintaining a testimony above reproach.
2. Honor those unknown servants who are currently shepherding God's people with integrity and holiness, whose ministries are bearing lasting fruit often not widely recognized.
3. Encourage all pastors and ministry leaders to persevere by faith in holiness, humility, and devotion to Christ, fixing their eyes on Jesus as the source of their faith and the guarantee of its completion.
4. Call upon all Southern Baptists to imitate such faith by pursuing lives of faithful endurance and to support and pray for their leaders (Hebrews 13:18).
5. Affirm that our confidence for finishing well is not our own power, but the power of God, "who is able to protect you from stumbling and to make you stand in the presence of his glory, without blemish and with great joy" (Jude 24).

## Resolution 9: On Appreciation for Bivocational and Volunteer Pastors

### Whereas Clauses

1. The Lord Jesus Christ has called pastors to shepherd his church faithfully through the preaching of the Word, prayer, discipleship, pastoral care, and leadership (Acts 20:28; 1 Timothy 4:11-12; Hebrews 13:17; 1 Peter 5:1-4).
2. A significant number of Southern Baptist churches are led by bivocational or volunteer pastors, who balance the demands of church ministry alongside marketplace employment and family responsibilities.
3. These pastors faithfully shepherd congregations, engage their communities, and advance the mission of Christ's church often through unseen acts of service, personal sacrifice, late nights, and long days.
4. Scripture affirms the dignity of labor and provides examples of ministry leaders like the apostle Paul who served the church bivocationally with faithful perseverance (Acts 18:1-3; 1 Thessalonians 2:9).
5. The terms "bivocational," "part-time," "covocational," and "volunteer" often refer only to compensation but do not adequately reflect the scope or responsibility of pastoral ministry (1 Timothy 5:18).
6. Many Southern Baptist churches and church plants, particularly in rural communities, small towns, and underserved areas, have been shepherded and strengthened through the faithful service of bivocational and volunteer pastors and their families.

### Resolved

1. Express heartfelt gratitude to Southern Baptist bivocational and volunteer pastors for their faithful and sacrificial service to Christ and his church.
2. Honor the commitment, perseverance, humility, and gospel faithfulness demonstrated by bivocational and volunteer pastors who labor diligently both in ministry and in the workplace (Romans 13:7).
3. Express appreciation for the wives and families of bivocational and volunteer pastors who support, sacrifice, encourage, and partner in ministry.
4. Exhort Southern Baptist churches to pray for, support, encourage, and care well for their pastors, recognizing the spiritual, emotional, mental, and physical demands of bivocational and volunteer ministry (1 Thessalonians 2:7-8; 2 Timothy 4:6).
5. Urge Southern Baptist churches, associations, state conventions, and entities to support the equipping and encouragement of bivocational and volunteer pastors.
6. Pray the Lord of the harvest would continue to raise up and send faithful pastors to proclaim the gospel, disciple believers, and strengthen churches in every community (Matthew 9:36-38).
7. Give thanks to God for the enduring ministry of Southern Baptist bivocational and volunteer pastors who have labored faithfully to advance the gospel across the generations.

## Resolution 10: On the Nature and Importance of the Physically Gathered Church in a Digital Age

### Whereas Clauses

1. Scripture presents the local church as an embodied assembly of baptized believers who covenant together for worship, discipleship, fellowship, and mission (Acts 2:41-47; Hebrews 10:24-25).
2. Christ entrusted the local church with baptism and the Lord's Supper as visible ordinances to be practiced within the gathered body (Matthew 28:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26).
3. Christ promises his active, validating presence over the collective decisions of his church when, acting as his witnesses, they gather together to prayerfully resolve matters in his name (Matthew 18:17-20).
4. Digital technologies and platforms provide new opportunities for gospel proclamation, biblical instruction, communication, and connection.
5. Technological developments also introduce challenges that may obscure the importance of the gathered church, of church membership, and of pastoral ministry, and may further confuse the goodness of embodied existence.
6. Local churches have sought to make wise use of available technologies for the sake of the gospel, while recognizing that such tools must never be confused with the local church itself.

### Resolved

1. Affirm that -- unless providentially hindered -- regular, embodied assembly is vital to equipping the saints for the work of ministry and building up the body of Christ, and that this biblical pattern is not fulfilled by digital participation alone (Ephesians 4:11-16; Hebrews 10:24-25).
2. Affirm the responsible use of digital technologies and platforms as aids to ministry, while recognizing that such tools cannot fulfill the biblical functions of the gathered church, cannot replace the personal ministry of pastors and members, and should be used to support connection to the embodied life of a faithful local church.
3. Affirm that baptism and the Lord's Supper are to be administered within the gathered church, under the oversight of pastors, in keeping with the New Testament witness, and not by virtual or digitally mediated substitutes.
4. Encourage local churches to exercise wisdom, integrity, and theological discernment as they teach and model meaningful membership, mutual accountability, and faithful participation in the embodied life of the church.
5. Reaffirm that a local church is the embodied assembly of baptized believers, gathered for his glory and gifted for service, and is not merely a virtual or technological experience, nor can any digital form, by itself, constitute a New Testament church (Romans 12:3-13; 1 Corinthians 12).

## Resolution 11: On the Office and Function of Pastor/Elder/Overseer

### Whereas Clauses

1. Scripture assigns the work of shepherding, overseeing, and teaching and preaching to the gathered church to qualified men appointed by the church to the office of pastor/elder/overseer (John 21:15-17; Acts 20:28; Ephesians 4:11; 1 Timothy 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9; 1 Peter 5:1-3).
2. The Baptist Faith and Message 2000 states that the church's "two scriptural offices are that of pastor/elder/overseer and deacon" and that "the office of pastor/elder/overseer is limited to men as qualified by Scripture" (Article VI).
3. The Southern Baptist Convention recently reaffirmed its confessional heritage that the New Testament terms "bishop," "elder," "overseer," and "pastor" refer to the same biblical office ("On the Southern Baptist Confessional Heritage of the Office of Bishop/Elder/Pastor," 2023).
4. Scripture teaches that women are equally created in the image of God, gifted by the Holy Spirit for meaningful service, and valuable in the work of the church (Genesis 1:27; Acts 2:17-18; 1 Corinthians 11:5, 12; Titus 2:3-5).
5. The Southern Baptist Convention has repeatedly affirmed both the equal dignity and value of women in service to the church ("On the Legacy and Responsibility of Women Fulfilling the Great Commission," 2023; "On the Dignity and Worth of Women," 2018).
6. Confusion has arisen in some Southern Baptist churches regarding the relationship between the title, office, and function of pastor, including the use of titles "pastor," "elder," or "overseer" for roles inconsistent with the Convention's articulated understanding of Scripture.
7. Southern Baptists have recognized that biblical terminology should be used carefully and consistently in order to promote doctrinal clarity, faithful cooperation, and unity among churches.

### Resolved

1. Reaffirm that the office of pastor/elder/overseer is limited to men as qualified by Scripture.
2. Affirm that the New Testament presents the pastoral office and the function of pastoral oversight of the church as inseparably connected.
3. Affirm that the function of teaching Scripture and preaching the Word to the gathered church is essentially connected to the office of pastor/elder/overseer and should not be separated from the qualifications for pastoral ministry.
4. Encourage Southern Baptist churches to maintain clarity and integrity in their ministerial titles and practices so that nomenclature is not used in ways that obscure or contradict the Convention's adopted statement of faith regarding the pastoral office; specifically, to use the titles "pastor," "elder," and "overseer" in a manner consistent with the biblical office described in Scripture and affirmed in The Baptist Faith and Message 2000.
5. Express gratitude for the indispensable service, discipleship, evangelism, missions work, and ministry contributions of women throughout Southern Baptist life and encourage churches to continue affirming and deploying women in biblically faithful ways.
6. Call pastors to teach and uphold the biblical pattern of church leadership, which includes exemplifying the qualifications of godly character and conduct prescribed in 1 Timothy 3:1-7 and Titus 1:5-9.
7. Commend this understanding to the churches of the Convention and to the entities and committees serving the Convention as reflective of the Southern Baptist Convention's confessional understanding of the office and function of pastor.
